# Product-stable epimorphisms of locally ringed spaces

Let $R$ be a non-trivial commutative ring. We discuss whether the category [$\LRS_R$](/category/LRS_R) of locally ringed spaces over $R$ has [finite-product-stable epimorphisms](/category-property/finite-product-stable_epimorphisms) or [product-stable epimorphisms](/category-property/product-stable_epimorphisms). The answer depends on $R$:

- For $R = \IZ$, the category does not have finite-product-stable epimorphisms ([Proposition 1](#proposition_1)).
- For a field $R = K$ that is not separably closed, the category does not have finite-product-stable epimorphisms ([Theorem 2](#theorem_2)).
- For a separably closed field $R = K$, the category has product-stable epimorphisms ([Theorem 9](#theorem_9)).

Since product-stable epimorphisms are in particular finite-product-stable, in each case either both properties hold or both fail.

For a point $y$ of a locally ringed space $Y$, we write $\m_y$ for the maximal ideal of $\O_{Y,y}$ and $\kappa(y)$ for its residue field. Throughout, we use the classification of epimorphisms in $\LRS_R$ (see [its entry](/category/LRS_R)): a morphism $f : X \to Y$ is an epimorphism iff $|f| : |X| \to |Y|$ is surjective and $f^\sharp : \O_Y(V) \to \O_X(|f|^*(V))$ is injective for every open subset $V \subseteq |Y|$. Recall that for a point $x \in |X|$ and a local section $s$ of $\O_Y$ defined near $|f|(x)$, the germ of $f^\sharp(s)$ at $x$ is $f^\sharp_x(s_{|f|(x)})$. In particular, if $|f|$ is surjective and every stalk map $f^\sharp_x$ is injective, then $f$ is an epimorphism: if $f^\sharp(s) = 0$ for some $s \in \O_Y(V)$, then for every $y \in V$ we choose $x \in |X|$ with $|f|(x) = y$ and get $f^\sharp_x(s_y) = 0$, hence $s_y = 0$, so that $s = 0$. The converse fails, which makes the proof of [Theorem 8](#theorem_8) non-trivial: the epimorphism in the proof of [Proposition 1](#proposition_1) has the stalk map $\IZ_{(2)} \to \IF_2$ at the point of $\Spec(\IF_2)$, which is not injective.

## The case of the integers

::: Proposition 1
The category $\LRS = \LRS_{\IZ}$ does not have finite-product-stable epimorphisms.
:::

::: Proof
The canonical morphism $f : \Spec(\IQ) \sqcup \Spec(\IF_2) \to \Spec(\IZ_{(2)})$ is an epimorphism: $|f|$ is bijective, and the maps on sections are injective, namely $\IZ_{(2)} \to \IQ \times \IF_2$ over the whole space and $\IQ \to \IQ$ over the generic point, which is open. Since $\LRS$ is [distributive](/category-property/distributive) and $\Spec : \CRing^\op \to \LRS$ preserves limits (being a right adjoint), the product of $f$ with $\Spec(\IZ/4)$ is the morphism
$$\Spec(\IQ \otimes \IZ/4) \sqcup \Spec(\IF_2 \otimes \IZ/4) \to \Spec(\IZ_{(2)} \otimes \IZ/4),$$
i.e. the morphism $\Spec(\IF_2) \to \Spec(\IZ/4)$ induced by the projection $\IZ/4 \to \IF_2$, using $\IQ \otimes \IZ/4 = 0$ and $\Spec(0) = \varnothing$. It is no epimorphism since its map on global sections $\IZ/4 \to \IF_2$ is not injective.
:::

## Products over a field

From now on, let $K$ be a field, and all tensor products are taken over $K$ unless indicated otherwise. We recall the construction of products in $\LRS_K$ from [Gillam](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.2139). Since a $K$-algebra structure on the structure sheaf of a locally ringed space $X$ is the same as a morphism $X \to \Spec(K)$, a product in $\LRS_K$ is a wide pullback over $\Spec(K)$ in $\LRS$. By Corollary 5 there, it is the localization (Section 2.2 there) of the corresponding limit in ringed spaces (see the proof of Theorem 4 there) at the prime ideals lying over the maximal ideals. Unwinding this construction, the product $P$ of a family $(X_i)_{i \in I}$ of locally ringed spaces over $K$ looks as follows.

- For $x = (x_i)_{i \in I} \in \prod_{i \in I} |X_i|$, let $A_x$ be the $K$-algebra $\bigotimes_{i \in I} \O_{X_i,x_i}$. For infinite $I$, this is the coproduct of commutative $K$-algebras, i.e. the filtered colimit of the tensor products over the finite subsets of $I$. Let $\bar{A}_x$ be the $K$-algebra $\bigotimes_{i \in I} \kappa(x_i)$, which is the quotient of $A_x$ by the ideal generated by the images of the ideals $\m_{x_i}$.
- The points of $P$ are the pairs $(x,\p)$, where $x \in \prod_{i \in I} |X_i|$ and $\p$ is a prime ideal of $A_x$ whose preimage in each $\O_{X_i,x_i}$ is $\m_{x_i}$. These are exactly the prime ideals containing the kernel of $A_x \to \bar{A}_x$, so that the points of $P$ over $x$ correspond to the prime ideals of $\bar{A}_x$. Since $\bar{A}_x$ is non-zero, there is at least one such point.
- For a finite subset $J \subseteq I$, open subsets $U_i \subseteq |X_i|$ for $i \in J$, and an element $b \in \bigotimes_{i \in J} \O_{X_i}(U_i)$, let $D(b)$ be the set of points $(x,\p)$ of $P$ with $x_i \in U_i$ for all $i \in J$ and $b_x \notin \p$, where $b_x \in A_x$ is the image of $b$ (obtained by taking germs). These sets form a basis of the topology of $|P|$.
- The stalk of $\O_P$ at $(x,\p)$ is the localization $(A_x)_{\p}$. For an open subset $W \subseteq |P|$, the sections $s \in \O_P(W)$ are the families $(s(x,\p))_{(x,\p) \in W}$ with $s(x,\p) \in (A_x)_{\p}$ that are locally fractions: every point of $W$ has a neighborhood $D(b) \subseteq W$ as above such that $s(x,\p) = a_x / b_x$ for all $(x,\p) \in D(b)$ and some $a \in \bigotimes_{i \in J} \O_{X_i}(U_i)$. In this case, we write $s = a/b$ on $D(b)$. The germ of $s$ at $(x,\p)$ is $s(x,\p)$.
- For $j \in I$, the projection $P \to X_j$ maps $(x,\p)$ to $x_j$, and its stalk map at $(x,\p)$ is the composite $\O_{X_j,x_j} \to A_x \to (A_x)_{\p}$.
- For a family of morphisms $f_i : X_i \to Y_i$, the product morphism $g \coloneqq \prod_{i \in I} f_i : P \to \prod_{i \in I} Y_i$ maps a point $(x,\p)$ to $(y,\varphi_x^*(\p))$, where $y_i \coloneqq |f_i|(x_i)$ and the $K$-algebra homomorphism $\varphi_x : A_y \to A_x$ is the tensor product of the stalk maps $(f_i)^\sharp_{x_i} : \O_{Y_i,y_i} \to \O_{X_i,x_i}$. The stalk map of $g$ at $(x,\p)$ is the local $K$-algebra homomorphism $(A_y)_{\varphi_x^*(\p)} \to (A_x)_{\p}$ induced by $\varphi_x$ (see the proof of Theorem 2 there).

For two locally ringed spaces $Y,Z$ over $K$, we write the points of $Y \times Z$ as triples $(y,z,\p)$, where $\p$ is a prime ideal of $A_{y,z} = \O_{Y,y} \otimes \O_{Z,z}$, and we have $\bar{A}_{y,z} = \kappa(y) \otimes \kappa(z)$.

## Fields that are not separably closed

We first describe the idea of the counterexample. We construct an epimorphism $f : X \to Y$ and a finite Galois extension $L/K$ such that $f \times \id_{\Spec(L)}$ is not an epimorphism. The counterexample is small: $X$ and $Y$ have two points each, $\Spec(L)$ has one point, and the products $X \times \Spec(L)$ and $Y \times \Spec(L)$ have $2 \, [L : K]$ points each. The space $|Y|$ has two points, and the stalk at its closed point is the local $K$-algebra $R$ of a "twisted node": the affine line over $L$, localized at $0$ and $1$, with the points $0$ and $1$ glued along a non-trivial automorphism $\sigma$ of $L$. As in [Proposition 1](#proposition_1), $X$ is the disjoint union of the open generic point of $Y$ (with its stalk) and the closed point of $Y$ (with its residue field $L$), and $f$ is the canonical morphism. After the base change to $L$, the line splits into several copies, and the point $0$ of each copy is glued to the point $1$ of another copy. So the local ring at a glued point has two branches. The stalk of $Y$ at its generic point only sees one of these branches, and we will construct a non-zero section that lives on the other branch and vanishes after pulling it back to $X \times \Spec(L)$.

::: Theorem 2
If $K$ is not separably closed, then $\LRS_K$ does not have finite-product-stable epimorphisms.
:::

::: Proof
Since $K$ is not separably closed, there is a finite Galois extension $L/K$ with $L \neq K$. Choose a $K$-automorphism $\sigma$ of $L$ with $\sigma \neq \id$.

**The rings.** Let $D \subseteq L(T)$ be the $L$-subalgebra of rational functions that are defined at $0$ and $1$, i.e. the localization of $L[T]$ at the set of polynomials that do not vanish at $0$ and $1$. It is a domain with evaluation $L$-algebra homomorphisms $D \to L$, $h \mapsto h(0)$ and $h \mapsto h(1)$, and $h \in D$ is a unit iff $h(0) \neq 0 \neq h(1)$. Let
$$R \coloneqq \{h \in D : h(1) = \sigma(h(0))\}.$$
Since $\sigma$ fixes $K$, $R$ is a $K$-subalgebra of $D$. It is a local $K$-algebra with maximal ideal
$$\m_R \coloneqq \{h \in D : h(0) = 0 = h(1)\}.$$
In fact, if $h \in R \setminus \m_R$, then $h(0) \neq 0$ and $h(1) = \sigma(h(0)) \neq 0$, so $h$ is a unit of $D$, and $h^{-1} \in R$ follows from
$$h^{-1}(1) = h(1)^{-1} = \sigma(h(0))^{-1} = \sigma(h(0)^{-1}) = \sigma(h^{-1}(0)).$$
The $K$-algebra homomorphism $\ev_0 : R \to L$, $h \mapsto h(0)$ has kernel $\m_R$, and it is surjective since $(1-T) \lambda + T \sigma(\lambda) \in R$ is mapped to $\lambda \in L$. Finally, let
$$D_0 \coloneqq L[T]_{\langle T \rangle} \subseteq L(T)$$
be the local $L$-subalgebra of rational functions that are defined at $0$, whose maximal ideal consists of the $h \in D_0$ with $h(0) = 0$. Then $R \subseteq D \subseteq D_0$.

**Identifying the tensor products.** Let $\Gamma \coloneqq \Gal(L/K)$, so that $\sigma \in \Gamma$. For every $L$-vector space $M$, we regard $M \otimes L$ as an $L$-vector space via the left factor $M$. Then there is an $L$-linear isomorphism
$$\textstyle \theta_M : M \otimes L \to \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} M, \quad m \otimes \lambda \mapsto (\tau(\lambda) \, m)_{\tau \in \Gamma}.$$
For $M = L$, this is the well-known isomorphism of $L$-algebras $L \otimes L \cong \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L$, and in general it is obtained from it by applying $M \otimes_L -$, where $L$ acts on $L \otimes L$ via the first factor. If $M$ is an $L$-algebra, it is an isomorphism of $L$-algebras. It is natural in $M$, and we will apply it to $M = D, \m_R, D_0, L$. Since $L$ is flat over $K$, we may regard $R \otimes L$ as a $K$-subalgebra of $D \otimes L$. Let $B$ be the $K$-subalgebra
$$\textstyle B \coloneqq \bigl\{h \in \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D : h_\tau(1) = \sigma(h_{\sigma^{-1} \tau}(0)) \text{ for all } \tau \in \Gamma\bigr\}$$
of $\prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D$. We claim that $\theta_D(R \otimes L) = B$.
For $h \in R$ and $\lambda \in L$, we have $\theta_D(h \otimes \lambda) = (\tau(\lambda) \, h)_{\tau \in \Gamma}$, which lies in $B$ because
$$\tau(\lambda) \, h(1) = \tau(\lambda) \, \sigma(h(0)) = \sigma\bigl(\sigma^{-1}(\tau(\lambda)) \, h(0)\bigr).$$
Hence, $\theta_D(R \otimes L) \subseteq B$. Both $K$-subalgebras contain $\theta_D(\m_R \otimes L) = \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} \m_R$. The $K$-algebra homomorphism
$$\textstyle \varphi : B \to \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L, \quad h \mapsto (h_\tau(0))_{\tau \in \Gamma}$$
has kernel $\prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} \m_R$, since $h_\tau(0) = 0$ for all $\tau$ implies $h_\tau(1) = \sigma(h_{\sigma^{-1} \tau}(0)) = 0$ for all $\tau$. Therefore, with $n \coloneqq [L : K] = \card(\Gamma)$, the $K$-vector space $B / \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} \m_R$ has dimension at most $\dim_K \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L = n^2$. On the other hand,
$$\textstyle \theta_D(R \otimes L) / \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} \m_R \cong (R \otimes L) / (\m_R \otimes L) \cong (R/\m_R) \otimes L \cong L \otimes L$$
has dimension $n^2$. It follows that $\theta_D(R \otimes L) = B$ and that $\varphi$ is surjective. Moreover, we have
$$\textstyle \varphi \circ \theta_D = \theta_L \circ (\ev_0 \otimes \id_L) : R \otimes L \to \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L,$$
since both sides map $h \otimes \lambda$ to $(\tau(\lambda) \, h(0))_{\tau \in \Gamma}$. From now on, we identify $R \otimes L$ with $B$ via $\theta_D$, and likewise $D_0 \otimes L$ with $\prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D_0$ via $\theta_{D_0}$ and $L \otimes L$ with $\prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L$ via $\theta_L$. Under these identifications, $\ev_0 \otimes \id_L : R \otimes L \to L \otimes L$ becomes $\varphi$. By the naturality of $\theta$, applied to the $L$-linear maps $D \hookrightarrow D_0$ and $\ev_0 : D_0 \to L$, the inclusion $R \otimes L \hookrightarrow D_0 \otimes L$ becomes the inclusion $B \subseteq \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D_0$, and $\ev_0 \otimes \id_L : D_0 \otimes L \to L \otimes L$ becomes the map $\prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D_0 \to \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L$, $h \mapsto (h_\tau(0))_{\tau \in \Gamma}$.

**The epimorphism.** Let $|Y| \coloneqq \{\eta,\xi\}$ be the Sierpiński space, whose open subsets are $\varnothing$, $\{\eta\}$, and $|Y|$. Define the sheaf $\O_Y$ of $K$-algebras by $\O_Y(|Y|) \coloneqq R$, $\O_Y(\{\eta\}) \coloneqq D_0$, and $\O_Y(\varnothing) \coloneqq 0$, where the restriction from $|Y|$ to $\{\eta\}$ is the inclusion $R \hookrightarrow D_0$. Its stalks $\O_{Y,\eta} = D_0$ and $\O_{Y,\xi} = R$ are local, so $Y = (|Y|,\O_Y)$ is a locally ringed space over $K$. Both residue fields identify with $L$ via evaluation at $0$: we have $\kappa(\eta) = D_0 / \langle T \rangle \cong L$ and $\kappa(\xi) = R/\m_R \cong L$. Let $X$ be the locally ringed space over $K$ with two points $x_\eta, x_\xi$, the discrete topology, and stalks $\O_{X,x_\eta} = D_0$ and $\O_{X,x_\xi} = L$. Let $f : X \to Y$ be the morphism with $|f|(x_\eta) \coloneqq \eta$ and $|f|(x_\xi) \coloneqq \xi$, whose map on sections is the $K$-algebra homomorphism
$$R \to D_0 \times L, \quad h \mapsto (h, h(0))$$
over $|Y|$ and $\id_{D_0}$ over $\{\eta\}$. These maps are compatible with the restrictions, and the stalk maps $\id_{D_0}$ and $\ev_0 : R \to L$ are local, so $f$ is indeed a morphism. Since $|f|$ is bijective and both maps on sections are injective, $f$ is an epimorphism.

**The base change to $L$.** Let $S \coloneqq \Spec(L)$, regarded as a locally ringed space over $K$. We will show that $f \times \id_S : X \times S \to Y \times S$ is not an epimorphism. Since $S$ has only one point, the points of $Y \times S$ are the pairs $(y,\p)$, where $y \in |Y|$ and $\p$ is a prime ideal of $\O_{Y,y} \otimes L$ that corresponds to a prime ideal of $\kappa(y) \otimes L \cong L \otimes L \cong \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L$. Since $\prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L$ is a finite product of fields, its prime ideals are the kernels of the projections $\prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L \to L$. For $y = \xi$, the quotient map $\O_{Y,\xi} \otimes L \to \kappa(\xi) \otimes L$ is $\ev_0 \otimes \id_L : R \otimes L \to L \otimes L$, i.e. $\varphi$. Hence, the points over $\xi$ are $(\xi,\p_\tau)$ for $\tau \in \Gamma$, where
$$\p_\tau \coloneqq \{h \in B : h_\tau(0) = 0\}$$
is the preimage under $\varphi$ of the kernel of the projection to the $\tau$-th factor.
For $y = \eta$, the quotient map $\O_{Y,\eta} \otimes L \to \kappa(\eta) \otimes L$ is $\ev_0 \otimes \id_L : D_0 \otimes L \to L \otimes L$, i.e. $h \mapsto (h_\tau(0))_{\tau \in \Gamma}$. Hence, the points over $\eta$ are $(\eta,\p'_\tau)$ for $\tau \in \Gamma$, where
$$\textstyle \p'_\tau \coloneqq \bigl\{h \in \prod_{\tau' \in \Gamma} D_0 : h_\tau(0) = 0\bigr\}.$$

**An open subset.** Let $b \in \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D$ be given by $b_{\id} \coloneqq 1 - T$, $b_\sigma \coloneqq T$, and $b_\tau \coloneqq 0$ for $\tau \notin \{\id,\sigma\}$. Then $b \in B$, i.e. $b_\tau(1) = \sigma(b_{\sigma^{-1} \tau}(0))$ for all $\tau \in \Gamma$: for $\tau = \sigma$, this condition reads $b_\sigma(1) = 1 = \sigma(b_{\id}(0))$, and for $\tau \neq \sigma$, both sides of it vanish: $b_\tau(1) = 0$ since $b_{\id}(1) = 1 - 1 = 0$, and $b_{\sigma^{-1} \tau}(0) = 0$ since $\sigma^{-1} \tau \neq \id$ and $b_\sigma(0) = 0$. So $b \in \O_Y(|Y|) \otimes \O_S(|S|)$, and $W \coloneqq D(b)$ is an open subset of $|Y \times S|$. The germ of $b$ at $\xi$ is $b \in B = \O_{Y,\xi} \otimes L$, and its germ at $\eta$ is $b \in \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D_0 = \O_{Y,\eta} \otimes L$, since the inclusion $R \otimes L \hookrightarrow D_0 \otimes L$ becomes the inclusion $B \subseteq \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D_0$. Hence, $(\xi,\p_\tau) \in W$ iff $b_\tau(0) \neq 0$ iff $\tau = \id$, and likewise $(\eta,\p'_\tau) \in W$ iff $\tau = \id$. Hence,
$$W = \{(\xi,\p_{\id}), (\eta,\p'_{\id})\}.$$

**B non-zero section.** Let $a \in \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D$ be given by $a_\sigma \coloneqq T(T-1)$ and $a_\tau \coloneqq 0$ for $\tau \neq \sigma$. Then $a \in \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} \m_R \subseteq B = \O_Y(|Y|) \otimes \O_S(|S|)$, so that $a$ defines the global section $a/1$ of $\O_{Y \times S}$ (a fraction on $D(1) = |Y \times S|$). Let $s \in \O_{Y \times S}(W)$ be the restriction of $a/1$, so that its germ at each point of $W$ is $a/1$. Its germ at $(\xi,\p_{\id})$ is $a/1 \in B_{\p_{\id}}$, which is non-zero: if $u \in B$ satisfies $ua = 0$, then $u_\sigma T(T-1) = 0$, hence $u_\sigma = 0$ since $D$ is a domain, and the condition $u_\sigma(1) = \sigma(u_{\id}(0))$ from the definition of $B$ gives $\sigma(u_{\id}(0)) = 0$, i.e. $u \in \p_{\id}$. Hence, $s \neq 0$.

**Its pullback vanishes.** Let $g \coloneqq f \times \id_S : X \times S \to Y \times S$. Since $\O_{X,x_\eta} = D_0 = \O_{Y,\eta}$, the points of $X \times S$ over $x_\eta$ are, as for $\eta$, the pairs $(x_\eta,\p'_\tau)$ for $\tau \in \Gamma$, and since $\O_{X,x_\xi} = \kappa(x_\xi) = L$, the points over $x_\xi$ are the pairs $(x_\xi,\q)$, where $\q$ is any prime ideal of $\O_{X,x_\xi} \otimes L = \kappa(x_\xi) \otimes L = \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L$, i.e. the kernel of a projection $\prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L \to L$. The stalk maps of $f$ are $\id_{D_0}$ and $\ev_0 : R \to L$, and $\ev_0 \otimes \id_L : R \otimes L \to L \otimes L$ becomes $\varphi$. By the description of product morphisms, $|g|$ maps $(x_\eta,\p'_\tau)$ to $(\eta,\p'_\tau)$ and $(x_\xi,\q)$ to $(\xi,\varphi^*(\q))$, which is $(\xi,\p_\tau)$ if $\q$ is the kernel of the projection to the $\tau$-th factor. Hence, $|g|^*(W)$ consists of the two points $(x_\eta,\p'_{\id})$ and $(x_\xi,\q)$, where $\q$ is the kernel of the projection to the $\id$-th factor.
The germ of $g^\sharp(s)$ at $(x_\eta,\p'_{\id})$ is $a/1 \in (\prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D_0)_{\p'_{\id}}$, which vanishes: the idempotent $e \in \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D_0$ with $e_{\id} = 1$ and $e_\tau = 0$ for $\tau \neq \id$ satisfies $e \notin \p'_{\id}$ and $ea = 0$, since $a_{\id} = 0$. The germ of $g^\sharp(s)$ at $(x_\xi,\q)$ is $\varphi(a)/1$, which vanishes since $\varphi(a) = (a_\tau(0))_{\tau \in \Gamma} = 0$. Thus, $g^\sharp(s) = 0$. So $g^\sharp$ is not injective, and $g$ is no epimorphism.
:::

## Separably closed fields

We now show that $\LRS_K$ has product-stable epimorphisms when $K$ is separably closed. Some of the auxiliary results hold for every field $K$, and we state them in this generality. The idea of the proof is as follows. Let $f : X \to Y$ be an epimorphism and let $Z$ be a locally ringed space over $K$. A section of $\O_{Y \times Z}$ is locally a fraction $a/b$ with $a,b \in \O_Y(U) \otimes \O_Z(V)$. If its pullback to $X \times Z$ vanishes, then the image of $a$ vanishes in suitable localizations of the $K$-algebras $\O_{X,x} \otimes \O_{Z,z}$. Since $K$ is separably closed, the elements we localize at are no zero divisors ([Lemma 4](#lemma_4)), so these localization maps are injective, and $a$ already vanishes after pulling it back along $f$, and since $f^\sharp$ is injective, $a$ vanishes near the point under consideration. To make this work, we need that the relevant points of $|Y|$ form an open subset ([Lemma 5](#lemma_5)).

::: Lemma 3
Let $F/K$ be a finitely generated field extension. (1) There is a natural number $r \geq 1$ such that for every field extension $E/K$, every nilpotent element $u \in E \otimes F$ satisfies $u^r = 0$. (2) If $K$ is separably closed, then for every field extension $E/K$, every zero divisor of $E \otimes F$ is nilpotent.
:::

::: Proof
Choose a transcendence basis $t = (t_1,\dotsc,t_d)$ of $F/K$. Since $F/K$ is finitely generated, $r \coloneqq [F : K(t)]$ is finite. Let $E/K$ be a field extension. The $E$-algebra $H \coloneqq E \otimes K(t)$ is the localization of $E \otimes K[t] = E[t]$ at the non-zero polynomials in $K[t]$, hence an $E$-subalgebra of $E(t)$ containing $E[t]$. So it is a domain with field of fractions $E(t)$. Since $E \otimes F \cong H \otimes_{K(t)} F$ and $\dim_{K(t)}(F) = r$, the $E$-algebra $E \otimes F$ is a free $H$-module of rank $r$. Therefore, the non-zero elements of $H$ are no zero divisors of $E \otimes F$, so that $E \otimes F$ is an $E$-subalgebra of its localization at $H \setminus \{0\}$. Since $E(t)$ is the field of fractions of $H$, this localization is
$$\Lambda \coloneqq E(t) \otimes_H (E \otimes F) \cong E(t) \otimes_{K(t)} F,$$
an $E(t)$-algebra of dimension $r$.

We first prove (1). If $u \in E \otimes F$ is nilpotent, then multiplication by $u$ is a nilpotent $E(t)$-linear endomorphism of the $r$-dimensional $E(t)$-vector space $\Lambda$. Hence, its $r$-th power vanishes, and we get $u^r = u^r \cdot 1 = 0$.

Now we prove (2). By [SP/00I7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00I7), the $E$-algebra $E \otimes F$ has a unique minimal prime ideal, i.e. its nilradical $N$ is a prime ideal. Since the non-zero elements of $H$ are no zero divisors of the non-zero $E$-algebra $E \otimes F$, they are not nilpotent, so $N$ does not meet $H \setminus \{0\}$. Hence, $N \Lambda$ is a prime ideal of $\Lambda$. Every prime ideal $\q$ of $\Lambda$ contains $N \Lambda$, which consists of nilpotent elements, and $\q$ is maximal, since $\Lambda / \q$ is a finite-dimensional domain over $E(t)$, hence a field. In particular, $N \Lambda$ is maximal, and therefore it is the only prime ideal of $\Lambda$. So every non-unit of $\Lambda$ is nilpotent. Now let $u \in E \otimes F$ be a zero divisor, say $uv = 0$ with $v \neq 0$. Since $E \otimes F$ is an $E$-subalgebra of $\Lambda$, $u$ is no unit of $\Lambda$, hence nilpotent.
:::

::: Lemma 4
Let $K$ be separably closed, and let $B,C$ be local commutative $K$-algebras with residue fields $\kappa_B,\kappa_C$. If the image of an element $u \in B \otimes C$ in $\kappa_B \otimes \kappa_C$ is not nilpotent, then $u$ is no zero divisor of $B \otimes C$.
:::

::: Proof
Let $v \in B \otimes C$ with $uv = 0$. We need to show that $v = 0$.

We first reduce to the case that $B$ and $C$ are Noetherian and have finitely generated residue fields. Choose finitely generated $K$-subalgebras $B_0 \subseteq B$ and $C_0 \subseteq C$ with $u,v \in B_0 \otimes C_0$. Recall that the $K$-algebra homomorphism $B_0 \otimes C_0 \to B \otimes C$ is injective, since we work over a field. By Hilbert's basis theorem, $B_0$ and $C_0$ are Noetherian. Let $B_1 \subseteq B$ be the $K$-subalgebra of all fractions $b/b'$ with $b,b' \in B_0$ and $b' \notin \m_B$. It is the localization of $B_0$ at the prime ideal $B_0 \cap \m_B$, hence a Noetherian local $K$-algebra whose maximal ideal consists of the fractions $b/b'$ as above with $b \in \m_B$. Since $b'$ is a unit of $B$, we have $b/b' \in \m_B$ iff $b \in \m_B$, so this maximal ideal is $B_1 \cap \m_B$. Since $B_1 \cap \m_B$ is the preimage of $\m_B$ under the inclusion $B_1 \hookrightarrow B$, the residue field $\kappa_{B_1}$ is a subfield of $\kappa_B$. It is a finitely generated field extension of $K$: every element of $\kappa_{B_1}$ is the residue class of a fraction $b/b'$ as above, i.e. a quotient of residue classes of elements of $B_0$, so that $\kappa_{B_1}$ is generated as a field by the residue classes of finitely many generators of the $K$-algebra $B_0$. Define $C_1 \subseteq C$ in the same way. Then $u,v \in B_1 \otimes C_1 \subseteq B \otimes C$, and the image of $u$ in $\kappa_{B_1} \otimes \kappa_{C_1} \subseteq \kappa_B \otimes \kappa_C$ is not nilpotent. So we may replace $B,C$ by $B_1,C_1$.

Assume $v \neq 0$. Write $v = \sum_\gamma b_\gamma \otimes c_\gamma$ with a $K$-basis $(c_\gamma)_\gamma$ of $C$. Then $v \in \m_B^n \otimes C$ iff $b_\gamma \in \m_B^n$ for all $\gamma$. Since $\bigcap_{n \geq 0} \m_B^n = 0$ by Krull's intersection theorem, there is some $n \geq 0$ with $v \in \m_B^n \otimes C$ and $v \notin \m_B^{n+1} \otimes C$. The quotient
$$(\m_B^n \otimes C) / (\m_B^{n+1} \otimes C) \cong (\m_B^n / \m_B^{n+1}) \otimes C$$
is a $(B \otimes C)$-module annihilated by $\m_B \otimes C$, hence a module over $(B \otimes C)/(\m_B \otimes C) = \kappa_B \otimes C$, and as such it is free, since $\m_B^n / \m_B^{n+1}$ is a free $\kappa_B$-module. Let $u'$ be the image of $u$ in $\kappa_B \otimes C$. The image of $v$ in this quotient is non-zero and annihilated by $u'$ with respect to the module structure. Looking at a non-zero coordinate, we find a non-zero $v' \in \kappa_B \otimes C$ with $u' v' = 0$. Applying the same argument to $u' v' = 0$ with the roles of the two factors exchanged, i.e. using the subspaces $\kappa_B \otimes \m_C^n$ of $\kappa_B \otimes C$, we find that the image of $u$ in $\kappa_B \otimes \kappa_C$ annihilates a non-zero element, i.e. it is a zero divisor. By [Lemma 3](#lemma_3), applied to $E = \kappa_B$ and $F = \kappa_C$, it is nilpotent, which contradicts our assumption.
:::

The assumption on $K$ in [Lemma 4](#lemma_4) is necessary. To see this, take $B = D_0$ and $C = L$ from the proof of [Theorem 2](#theorem_2). With the identifications made there, we have $B \otimes C = \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D_0$ and $\kappa_B \otimes \kappa_C = \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} L$, with the map $h \mapsto (h_\tau(0))_{\tau \in \Gamma}$ between them. The idempotent $e \in \prod_{\tau \in \Gamma} D_0$ with $e_{\id} = 1$ and $e_\tau = 0$ for $\tau \neq \id$ is a zero divisor, since $e \neq 1$ (as $\sigma \neq \id$). Its image $(e_\tau(0))_{\tau \in \Gamma}$ is a non-zero idempotent, hence not nilpotent.

::: Lemma 5
Let $Y$ be a locally ringed space over $K$, let $U \subseteq |Y|$ be open, let $E/K$ be a field extension, and let $b \in \O_Y(U) \otimes E$. For $y \in U$, let $b(y) \in \kappa(y) \otimes E$ be the image of $b$. Then the set of all $y \in U$ such that $b(y)$ is not nilpotent is open.
:::

::: Proof
There is a finitely generated subfield $F \subseteq E$ over $K$ such that $b$ lies in the $K$-subalgebra $\O_Y(U) \otimes F$ of $\O_Y(U) \otimes E$. Then $b(y)$ lies in the $K$-subalgebra $\kappa(y) \otimes F$ of $\kappa(y) \otimes E$. So we may assume that $E/K$ is finitely generated. By [Lemma 3](#lemma_3) (1), applied to $E/K$ and $\kappa(y)/K$, there is some $r \geq 1$ independent of $y$ such that $b(y)$ is nilpotent iff $b(y)^r = 0$. Choose a $K$-basis $(c_\gamma)_\gamma$ of $E$ and write
$$\textstyle b^r = \sum_\gamma a_\gamma \otimes c_\gamma \in \O_Y(U) \otimes E$$
with $a_\gamma \in \O_Y(U)$, almost all zero. Then $b(y)^r = \sum_\gamma a_\gamma(y) \otimes c_\gamma$, where $a_\gamma(y) \in \kappa(y)$ is the residue class of the germ $(a_\gamma)_y$. This element vanishes iff $a_\gamma(y) = 0$ for all $\gamma$. So $b(y)$ is not nilpotent iff $a_\gamma(y) \neq 0$ for some $\gamma$, and it suffices to show that each set $\{y \in U : a_\gamma(y) \neq 0\}$ is open. Indeed, $a_\gamma(y) \neq 0$ means that $(a_\gamma)_y$ is a unit of $\O_{Y,y}$, and then $a_\gamma$ is invertible on a neighborhood of $y$.
:::

::: Lemma 6
If $(f_i : X_i \to Y_i)_{i \in I}$ is a family of morphisms in $\LRS_K$ such that each $|f_i|$ is surjective, then $|\prod_{i \in I} f_i|$ is surjective.
:::

::: Proof
Let $(y,\q)$ be a point of $\prod_{i \in I} Y_i$, and let $\bar{\q}$ be the corresponding prime ideal of $\bar{A}_y$. Choose $x_i \in |X_i|$ with $|f_i|(x_i) = y_i$, and let $x \coloneqq (x_i)_{i \in I}$. The stalk maps of the $f_i$ induce field extensions $\kappa(y_i) \to \kappa(x_i)$ and hence a $K$-algebra homomorphism $\bar{\varphi}_x : \bar{A}_y \to \bar{A}_x$, which is induced by $\varphi_x : A_y \to A_x$. It makes $\bar{A}_x$ a free $\bar{A}_y$-module: if we choose a $\kappa(y_i)$-basis of $\kappa(x_i)$ containing $1$ for each $i$, then the tensor products of basis elements, almost all of which are $1$, form a basis. Since this basis is non-empty, $\bar{\varphi}_x$ is faithfully flat, so that by [SP/00HQ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00HQ) there is a prime ideal $\bar{\p}$ of $\bar{A}_x$ with $\bar{\varphi}_x^*(\bar{\p}) = \bar{\q}$. Its preimage $\p$ in $A_x$ satisfies $\varphi_x^*(\p) = \q$, i.e. the product morphism maps $(x,\p)$ to $(y,\q)$.
:::

::: Lemma 7
If $Y,Z$ are locally ringed spaces over $K$ and $Z$ is non-empty, then the projection $Y \times Z \to Y$ is an epimorphism.
:::

::: Proof
Let $\pi : Y \times Z \to Y$ be the projection. For $y \in |Y|$, choose $z \in |Z|$. By the description of products, there is a point of $Y \times Z$ over $(y,z)$. So $|\pi|$ is surjective. The stalk map of $\pi$ at a point $(y,z,\p)$ is the composite $\O_{Y,y} \to A_{y,z} \to (A_{y,z})_{\p}$. It is a local $K$-algebra homomorphism, since $\p$ lies over $\m_y$, and it is flat, since $\O_{Z,z}$ is flat over $K$ and localizations are flat. Hence, it is faithfully flat ([SP/00HR](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00HR)) and in particular injective ([SP/05CK](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/05CK)). Hence, $\pi$ is an epimorphism.
:::

::: Theorem 8
If $K$ is separably closed, then $\LRS_K$ has finite-product-stable epimorphisms.
:::

::: Proof
By the definition of [finite-product-stable epimorphisms](/category-property/finite-product-stable_epimorphisms), we need to show that for every epimorphism $f : X \to Y$ and every locally ringed space $Z$ over $K$, the morphism
$$g \coloneqq f \times \id_Z : P \coloneqq X \times Z \to Y \times Z \eqqcolon Q$$
is an epimorphism. By [Lemma 6](#lemma_6), $|g|$ is surjective. It remains to show that $g^\sharp$ is injective. So let $W \subseteq |Q|$ be open, let $s \in \O_Q(W)$ with $g^\sharp(s) = 0$, and let $q_0 = (y_0,z_0,\q_0) \in W$. We show that the germ of $s$ at $q_0$ vanishes.

By the description of products, there are open neighborhoods $U$ of $y_0$ and $V$ of $z_0$ and elements $a,b \in \O_Y(U) \otimes \O_Z(V)$ such that $q_0 \in D(b) \subseteq W$ and $s = a/b$ on $D(b)$. Let $a',b' \in \O_Y(U) \otimes \O_{Z,z_0}$ be the images of $a,b$, obtained by taking germs at $z_0$. For $y \in U$, we write $a'_y,b'_y \in A_{y,z_0} = \O_{Y,y} \otimes \O_{Z,z_0}$ for the germs and $b'(y) \in \bar{A}_{y,z_0} = \kappa(y) \otimes \kappa(z_0)$ for the residue class of $b'_y$. Then a point $(y,z_0,\q)$ with $y \in U$ lies in $D(b)$ iff $b'_y \notin \q$, and in this case the germ of $s$ at this point is $a'_y / b'_y$.

Let $U' \subseteq U$ be the set of all $y \in U$ such that $b'(y)$ is not nilpotent. By [Lemma 5](#lemma_5), applied to the image of $b'$ in $\O_Y(U) \otimes \kappa(z_0)$, $U'$ is open. It contains $y_0$: since $q_0 \in D(b)$, we have $b'_{y_0} \notin \q_0$, so $b'(y_0)$ does not lie in the corresponding prime ideal of $\kappa(y_0) \otimes \kappa(z_0)$ and is therefore not nilpotent.

Choose a $K$-basis $(c_\gamma)_\gamma$ of $\O_{Z,z_0}$ and write $a' = \sum_\gamma a_\gamma \otimes c_\gamma$ with $a_\gamma \in \O_Y(U)$, almost all zero. We show that $a_\gamma|_{U'} = 0$ for all $\gamma$. Then $(a_\gamma)_{y_0} = 0$ for all $\gamma$, so $a'_{y_0} = 0$, and the germ $a'_{y_0} / b'_{y_0}$ of $s$ at $q_0$ vanishes. Since $f$ is an epimorphism, $f^\sharp : \O_Y(U') \to \O_X(|f|^*(U'))$ is injective, so it suffices to show that $f^\sharp(a_\gamma|_{U'}) = 0$, i.e. that its germ $f^\sharp_x((a_\gamma)_y)$ vanishes for every $x \in |f|^*(U')$, where $y \coloneqq |f|(x)$.

So let $x \in |f|^*(U')$ and $y \coloneqq |f|(x)$. Let $\psi$ be the $K$-algebra homomorphism $f^\sharp_x \otimes \id_{\O_{Z,z_0}} : A_{y,z_0} \to A_{x,z_0}$. It induces the $K$-algebra homomorphism $\bar{\psi} : \kappa(y) \otimes \kappa(z_0) \to \kappa(x) \otimes \kappa(z_0)$, which is injective since $\kappa(y) \to \kappa(x)$ is injective and we work over a field. The residue class of $\psi(b'_y)$ in $\kappa(x) \otimes \kappa(z_0)$ is $\bar{\psi}(b'(y))$, which is not nilpotent, since $b'(y)$ is not nilpotent by $y \in U'$ and $\bar{\psi}$ is injective. Choose a prime ideal $\bar{\p}$ of $\kappa(x) \otimes \kappa(z_0)$ that does not contain it, and let $p = (x,z_0,\p)$ be the corresponding point of $P$, i.e. $\p$ is the preimage of $\bar{\p}$ in $A_{x,z_0}$. By the description of product morphisms, $|g|(p) = (y,z_0,\psi^*(\p))$, which lies in $D(b)$ since $\psi(b'_y) \notin \p$. The germ of $g^\sharp(s)$ at $p$ is
$$\psi(a'_y) / \psi(b'_y) \in (A_{x,z_0})_{\p}.$$
Since $g^\sharp(s) = 0$, there is some $u \in A_{x,z_0} \setminus \p$ with $u \, \psi(a'_y) = 0$. The image of $u$ in $\kappa(x) \otimes \kappa(z_0)$ is not contained in $\bar{\p}$, so it is not nilpotent. By [Lemma 4](#lemma_4), $u$ is no zero divisor, and therefore
$$\textstyle 0 = \psi(a'_y) = \sum_\gamma f^\sharp_x((a_\gamma)_y) \otimes c_\gamma$$
in $\O_{X,x} \otimes \O_{Z,z_0}$, which implies $f^\sharp_x((a_\gamma)_y) = 0$ for all $\gamma$.
:::

We now pass from finite to arbitrary products. The idea is that every section of an infinite product is locally pulled back from a finite subproduct, and the projection onto it is an epimorphism by [Lemma 7](#lemma_7).

::: Theorem 9
If $K$ is separably closed, then $\LRS_K$ has product-stable epimorphisms.
:::

::: Proof
Let $(f_i : X_i \to Y_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of epimorphisms, and let
$$\textstyle g \coloneqq \prod_{i \in I} f_i : P \coloneqq \prod_{i \in I} X_i \to \prod_{i \in I} Y_i \eqqcolon Q.$$
If some $X_i$ is empty, then so is $Y_i$, since $|f_i|$ is surjective. Then $P$ and $Q$ are empty, and $g$ is an isomorphism. So we may assume that every $X_i$ is non-empty. By [Lemma 6](#lemma_6), $|g|$ is surjective. For a finite subset $J \subseteq I$, let $P_J \coloneqq \prod_{i \in J} X_i$, $Q_J \coloneqq \prod_{i \in J} Y_i$, and $g_J \coloneqq \prod_{i \in J} f_i : P_J \to Q_J$, and let $\pi_J : P \to P_J$ and $\pi'_J : Q \to Q_J$ be the projections, so that the following diagram commutes:

$$
\begin{CD}
P @>{g}>> Q \\
@V{\pi_J}VV @VV{\pi'_J}V \\
P_J @>>{g_J}> Q_J
\end{CD}
$$

By [Theorem 8](#theorem_8), $g_J$ is an epimorphism. Moreover, $\pi_J$ is an epimorphism by [Lemma 7](#lemma_7): it is the projection $P_J \times \prod_{i \in I \setminus J} X_i \to P_J$, and the second factor is non-empty by the description of products, since every $X_i$ is non-empty.

Now let $W \subseteq |Q|$ be open, let $s \in \O_Q(W)$ with $g^\sharp(s) = 0$, and let $q \in W$. By the description of products, there are a finite subset $J \subseteq I$, open subsets $U_i \subseteq |Y_i|$ for $i \in J$, and elements $a,b \in \bigotimes_{i \in J} \O_{Y_i}(U_i)$ such that $q \in D(b) \subseteq W$ and $s = a/b$ on $D(b)$. Let $D_J(b) \subseteq |Q_J|$ be the corresponding basic open subset of $|Q_J|$, and let $s_J \coloneqq a/b \in \O_{Q_J}(D_J(b))$. By the construction of products, $\pi'_J$ maps $(y,\q)$ to $(y|_J, \q \cap A_{y|_J})$, where we regard $A_{y|_J}$ as a $K$-subalgebra of $A_y$, and its stalk maps are induced by the inclusions $A_{y|_J} \hookrightarrow A_y$. Hence, $D(b) = |\pi'_J|^*(D_J(b))$ and $s|_{D(b)} = (\pi'_J)^\sharp(s_J)$. Hence,
$$\pi_J^\sharp(g_J^\sharp(s_J)) = g^\sharp((\pi'_J)^\sharp(s_J)) = g^\sharp(s|_{D(b)}) = 0.$$
Since $\pi_J$ is an epimorphism, this implies $g_J^\sharp(s_J) = 0$, and since $g_J$ is an epimorphism, $s_J = 0$. Hence, $s|_{D(b)} = 0$, and in particular the germ of $s$ at $q$ vanishes.
:::
